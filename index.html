The idea of the universe being made of elements was first suggested by Aristotle — the eldest of the three Peverell brothers from the Tale of the Three Brothers. You may know him as the original owner of the elder wand from the tale of the Deathly Hallows. During this Greek wizard’s time, elements we know today, such as gold, had already been discovered and used. However, due to lack of knowledge, Aristotle only proposed four elements: Fire, Water, Earth, and Air.

Fast forwarding centuries later, when elements had already been named and discovered, slytherin Antoine Lavoisier provided a classification of 33 elements based on properties like metals and nonmetals. On top of that, he had researched on the role of oxygen in combustion and respiration, and even formulated the law of conservation of mass. He also developed a modern system used for naming chemical compounds, showing just how cunning and resourceful slytherins are.

Not long after, Professor Dobereiner — a potions teacher at Hogwarts — classified triads of elements, grouping them by their respective chemical properties. In this organization, the average atomic mass of the outer elements equates to that of the middle element. He continues to demonstrate the qualities of courage and determination both in and out of the classroom, being the perfect example of a gryffindor.

Following him was John Newlands - a hufflepuff - who introduced the Law of Octaves in 1864. Recognizing the pattern among the masses of elements, he exxemplifies the patience and hard work that lies within a Hufflepuff.

Best known when discussing the history of the periodic table, Dmitri Mendeleev created the very magical artifact we know today - the Periodic Table. As a Ravenclaw, his wit, wisdom, and creativity carried his discoveries and work, making his work both excellent and magical.
